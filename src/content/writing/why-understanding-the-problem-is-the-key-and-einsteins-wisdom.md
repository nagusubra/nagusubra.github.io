## 50 days of problem hunting !🎯

![](/posts/why-understanding-the-problem-is-the-key-and-einsteins-wisdom/img-01.png)

This quote always struck me as profound, but honestly, I couldn't relate to it for the longest time. As an engineer, it made no sense to me how five minutes could ever be enough to solve anything! My instinct has always been to jump straight into "_how_" I would solve the problem. It took me years of working on various engineering projects, hackathons, and competitions to fully grasp the deeper truth behind this idea.

I used to believe that as long as the solution was technically advanced, efficient, and feasible, it was golden. But time and experience have taught me that a technically brilliant solution is worthless if it doesn't address a clear, well-defined problem that people actually care about. No matter how great your solution is, without desirability and a simple, concrete problem statement, it can crumble. Stubbornly sticking to a solution can also lead you into the sunk-cost fallacy, where ego and attachments prevent you from adapting.

![](/posts/why-understanding-the-problem-is-the-key-and-einsteins-wisdom/img-02.jpg)

That's when I realized what Einstein was really getting at: the 55 minutes is about understanding the **_"why"_**behind the problem. When you truly know why a problem exists, the rest falls into place. Before diving into solutions, it's essential to deeply understand the backstory, identify key challenges, know the impact of leaving it unsolved, frame the problem statement and validate your problem. If validation shows that it's not a _"real problem"_, don't breakdown or go overboard just **_PIVOT!_** It's a tough pill to swallow, but it's a quintessential step towards meaningful success.

Once you've validated the problem, the rest of the journey is paved for you. The problem vaidation will guide you to start identifying your target customer segment, evaluating the adoption curve, and conducting competitor analysis — all the way to scoping out a solution. Hey, did you see that, you get the detailed scope! Its an agile engineer's dream, **a crisp list of requirements and features that shows the unique value of your solution.** Great, now spend those 5 mins to develop your solution.

![](/posts/why-understanding-the-problem-is-the-key-and-einsteins-wisdom/img-03.jpg)

Credits: [mechanicalengineeringmemes](https://www.instagram.com/mechanicalengineeringmemes/)

I could probably write a dozen Medium posts about how this shift in perspective has transformed my approach to problem-solving. But the truth is, I didn't fully resonate with Einstein's quote until I lived it — spending 55 minutes thinking through problems instead of rushing to solutions.

So, I'm embarking on a personal 50-day challenge. The goal? To identify problems, craft problem statements, validate them, and assess desirability, viability, and feasibility — without immediately jumping to solve them. Over the next 50 days, I'll identify **one problem every 10 days** and spend **"90% of my time"** on the critical steps that lead to real solutions. I'm holding off on solving them for now; that's for future me to decide based on what I learn from the validation process.

**I think I will end on "WHY " am I doing this? 😉**

> **To train my brain to prioritize purpose over solutions and to deepen my understanding of the problem space before leaping into action.**

I believe that engaging with the community not only spreads knowledge but also deepens my own understanding. So, stay tuned as I share my journey and the 5 problems I'll be discovering over the next 50 days!
